# Encore Cup '26 — Strategic District Audit
**Gap Inc. · District Loyalty Tournament · June 2026 (5 Weeks)**  
**Prepared by:** Benn Pattara  
**Data source:** Final weekly cashier exports · [Live dashboard](https://bennpattara.com/encore-cup)

---

## Executive Summary

Encore Cup drove engagement and surfaced real performance signal — but the district **missed its 1.80% conversion goal**, closing at **1.40%** across **32,210 transactions** and **452 cards landed**.

The diagnosis is not traffic. It is **coaching and conversion floor**.

High-volume stores processed thousands of transactions while large segments of their cashier base **landed zero applications across five full weeks**. Among cashiers who did land at least one application, many could not sustain goal-level conversion. The district had activity-shaped outcomes without a competency-shaped floor.

**Bottom line:** Encore Cup worked as a field pilot and diagnostic. What converts engagement into goal is a **tiered coaching structure** — not another blanket participation push.

---

## Methodology & Metric Definitions

| Term | Definition | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Landed ≥1** | Cashier processed **at least one application** during the 5-week tournament | Conversion outcome occurred at least once |
| **At goal** | Cashier **season rate** (total cards ÷ total transactions) meets or exceeds store June goal | Sustained competency, not a one-off |
| **Zero landed** | Cashier processed **zero applications** across all 5 weeks | Confirmed non-conversion — cause requires segmentation |
| **Exposure proxy** | Total transactions handled by a cashier across 5 weeks | Separates low-opportunity zeros from high-opportunity zeros |

**Important data limitation:** We do not have shift-count or hours-worked in the export. A cashier with 3 shifts and 0 cards is not the same as one with 20 shifts and 0 cards. Until shift data is added, transaction volume is used as a conservative exposure proxy. **We do not claim to know whether zero-landed cashiers "never asked"** — only that they **never landed an application**.

**What this means operationally:**

2. **Streets @ Southpoint** — highest volume in the underperformer group (4,083 txns) but only **12% of cashiers at goal** despite **64% landing at least one application**. This is the clearest **competency leak** in the district: people proved they *can* convert, but the store cannot sustain it.

3. **Green Hills** — highest landed rate among underperformers (69%) but still finished at 0.98%. Classic false-progress pattern: activity without competency.

## Three Actionable Insights

### 1. The district missed on capability, not traffic

32,210 transactions produced 1.40% conversion against a 1.80% goal. High-volume stores (Streets, Avalon, Lenox) moved significant traffic but finished well below goal because large portions of their cashier base never landed an application — and those who did often could not sustain goal-level performance. **Coaching must target the floor, not the register.**

### 2. Zero-landed cashiers are the single largest drag — and many had real opportunity

88 cashiers (40.6%) landed zero applications across five weeks. 62 handled 20+ transactions; 43 handled 50+. At Avalon alone, 8 cashiers with 30+ transactions each landed zero cards. We cannot confirm these associates never asked — but we can confirm they **never converted**, despite meaningful exposure. That is the highest-priority coaching cohort in the data.

### 3. Winners built aligned floors; underperformers created false progress

Stores that beat goal (Crabtree, Hanes, Short Pump) had smaller participation-to-competency gaps (20–28 pts). Underperformers showed high landed rates with low at-goal rates — Streets at a **52-point leak** (64% landed vs 12% at goal). The district was rewarding activity-shaped outcomes without measuring whether the team could **sustain** goal-level conversion.

---

## Three Actionable Recommendations

### 1. Implement a tiered coaching protocol — not one blanket fix

| Tier | Who | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Urgent** | Zero landed + 30+ txns | 1:1 coaching, weekly check-in, manager ride-along |
| **B — Skill** | Landed ≥1 but below goal | Pitch quality, close rate, consistency coaching |
| **C — Exposure** | Zero landed + <20 txns | Scheduling / opportunity review before accountability conversation |

This respects what the data actually proves. Tier A is accountability. Tier B is skill. Tier C is exposure. Treating all three the same wastes coaching effort.

### 2. Change the district scorecard

Replace primary people KPI from "% who opened" to:

1. **% cashiers at goal** (competency)
2. **Participation-to-competency gap** (landed ≥1 minus at goal)
3. **Zero-landed high-exposure count** per store, tracked weekly

Rate and card count stay on the board. But the people metrics should measure **sustained performance**, not one-time conversion.

### 3. Pilot a replicable "Crabtree Profile" as the district standard

| Target | Standard |
|---|---|
| Landed ≥1 | ≥55% of cashier base |
| Participation-to-competency gap | ≤10 points |
| Top-3 concentration | ≤50% of store cards |

Crabtree is the replicable template: broad participation with cards spread across the floor. Short Pump won on rate; the district standard should pair top-performer excellence with a wider competency floor.

**Next measurement build:** Add shift-count or hours-worked to weekly exports so Tier A/B/C assignment is based on confirmed opportunity, not transaction proxy alone.

## Data Limitations & Next Build

| Gap | Impact | Recommended Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No shift/hours data | Cannot distinguish "never had opportunity" from "had opportunity, never converted" with certainty | Add shift count to weekly export; filter coaching lists to 8+ shifts |
| Cannot confirm "asked" vs "landed" | Participation metric is outcome-based only | Pair tracker data with brief field pulse on pitch frequency for Tier B |
| Weekly cashier roster turnover | 162–169 cashiers/week vs 217 unique season — roster is fluid | Track cohort persistence week-over-week in next tournament |
